# Dana–Seong-ho rivalry guard

## Authority

- Status: `APPROVED_CHARACTER_GUARD`
- Confirmed by the user: 2026-07-30
- Scope: every outline, brief, draft, revision, relationship audit, and state
  update containing both Dana and Seong-ho

This guard clarifies the existing current-setting facts that Dana and Seong-ho
are athletic rivals and everyday antagonists and that their relationship does
not lead to romance. It does not change the spring injury event.

## Relationship foundation

- They are a same-type-friction pair: both are athletic, quick-tempered,
  competitive, action-first, protective of weaker children, reluctant to
  advertise kindness, and unwilling to lose face.
- Each recognizes their own irritating traits in the other. Similarity makes
  them collide more often rather than making them openly affectionate.
- Their normal rhythm is reciprocal: athletic competition, taunts, record
  comparisons, interrupted boasting, and immediate counterattacks.
- Seong-ho may call Dana `도릴라`. Dana must answer rather than passively
  absorb his teasing. Do not turn their rivalry into one-sided bullying.
- Both may protect or help others in similar ways while accusing the other of
  showing off. Shared morality does not require verbal reconciliation.

## S4 injury application

- Seong-ho is injured while helping a younger student during evacuation. This
  choice works because Dana would likely have made the same choice.
- Dana's guilt temporarily makes the relationship asymmetrical. She watches
  Seong-ho's ankle and route without explaining why.
- Dana expresses concern through cleared obstacles, practical interference,
  complaints about crutch use, and sharper-than-usual replies. Do not use
  tender nursing, a private heart-to-heart, or an uncharacteristically meek
  apology.
- Seong-ho finds quiet or deferential Dana more disturbing than combative Dana.
  He restarts the `도릴라` taunts and athletic challenge.
- Dana answering immediately is the sign that their relationship is recovering.
  Being able to growl at each other again is the payoff.

## Carry-forward state

- From S5 onward, restore reciprocal teasing, athletic competition, and
  argument as the default.
- Dana may retain responsibility for Seong-ho's injury without treating him as
  fragile or becoming his caretaker.
- Seong-ho may retain the cast experience without becoming gentle, grateful,
  submissive, or emotionally exclusive toward Dana.
- Their rivalry can produce teamwork, mutual recognition, or protection under
  pressure, but it returns to bickering once the immediate danger passes.

## Hard non-romance boundary

- Never frame Dana and Seong-ho as a future couple.
- The current setting's phrase that Seong-ho unconsciously likes Dana is
  operationally a broad fondness and grudging recognition, not latent romance
  or future romantic attraction.
- Do not use:
  - jealousy over another person
  - blushing or heartbeat emphasis
  - accidental-touch tension
  - private tender exclusivity
  - classmates shipping them
  - couple-coded quarrels
  - near-confession or misunderstood confession
  - a temporary separation whose emotional grammar resembles a breakup
  - narration implying they will understand their feelings later
- When a scene risks romantic interpretation, add the wider school or athletic
  context and restore reciprocal competition before the scene ends.

## Validation questions

1. Is the teasing reciprocal?
2. Does shared protectiveness arise from similar character rather than romantic
   devotion?
3. After a serious beat, does their ordinary competitive rhythm recover?
4. Could a reader reasonably read the scene as future-couple foreshadowing?
   If yes, revise it.
